## Flawless serving carries Alcaraz past Italian thunder

Carlos Alcaraz sent an unmistakable message to the indoor tennis circuit on Thursday afternoon, 29 October, dismantling former Wimbledon finalist Matteo Berrettini 6-3, 6-4 in the Round of 16 at the Erste Bank Open. Playing before a packed crowd inside the historic Wiener Stadthalle, the 23-year-old Spaniard delivered an exhibition of controlled aggression, requiring just 78 minutes to advance to Friday's quarter-finals.

Berrettini arrived armed with his trademark cannon serve, but Alcaraz neutralized the Italian's greatest weapon from the very first game. Standing deep behind the baseline, the World No. 2 returned with relentless depth, manufacturing the opening set's sole break in the fourth game with an exquisite backhand passing shot on the run. On his own delivery, Alcaraz was virtually unplayable: he won 84 percent of his first-serve points, landed seven aces, and did not concede a single break point across ten service games.

## Zverev joins Alcaraz in loaded Vienna quarter-finals

The second set followed a similar script, with Alcaraz seizing an immediate break in the third game courtesy of a delicate drop shot followed by an angled forehand volley. From there, the four-time Grand Slam champion closed out the contest with calm authority. The victory extends Alcaraz's head-to-head advantage over Berrettini to 4-1 and reinforces his status as the tournament frontrunner.

Joining Alcaraz in the last eight is top seed Alexander Zverev, who recovered from his exhausting three-hour opening-round marathon against Martin Landaluce to defeat American Marcos Giron 6-2, 7-5. With the ATP Finals in Turin fast approaching, Vienna's fast indoor courts are producing top-tier drama as the world's elite fine-tune their games for the season-ending championships.
