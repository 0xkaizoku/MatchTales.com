## Magic under the lights on the Lancashire coast

English football witnessed one of its most romantic and staggering cup upsets on Tuesday night, 27 October, as fourth-division Fleetwood Town knocked Premier League title co-leaders Arsenal out of the Carabao Cup. Highbury Stadium on the windy Fylde coast rocked to its foundations as 5,124 spectators roared the Cod Army across 120 minutes of sheer defiance and a 5-3 penalty shootout victory. Aaron Loupalo-Bi sent the home terraces into bedlam four minutes before the interval, rising unmarked to power a downward header past David Raya from a corner.

Mikel Arteta turned to his expensively assembled bench in the second half, introducing Viktor Gyokeres to spearhead the rescue mission. The Swedish international leveled proceedings in the 76th minute with a crisp low finish into the far corner, but Arsenal's numerical and territorial dominance vanished eight minutes later. Teenage substitute Max Dowman received a straight red card for an out-of-control tackle in midfield, swinging belief firmly back toward the League Two battlers.

## Harrington heroics seal history in the shootout

Fleetwood held firm through seven frantic minutes of stoppage time to force penalties, where goalkeeper David Harrington etched his name into coastal folklore. After Fleetwood converted their first two attempts, Harrington guessed correctly to his right, palming away Bukayo Saka's fierce drive. Ryan Broom calmly slotted the decisive fifth kick into the roof of the net, prompting an emotional pitch invasion as Fleetwood secured a quarter-final berth for the first time in their 118-year history.

With Manchester City facing Brighton and Liverpool hosting Chelsea in Wednesday night blockbusters, Fleetwood Town stand as the beacon of what makes knockout football magical. Arsenal depart with harsh questions about squad depth, while Lancashire celebrates an unforgettable triumph of grit over glamour.
