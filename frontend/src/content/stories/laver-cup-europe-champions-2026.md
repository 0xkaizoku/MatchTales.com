## Why this one matters

The 9th edition of the Laver Cup concluded on Sunday night with the highest stakes tennis can offer: a winner-take-all 12th match on the black court at London's O2 Arena. Team Europe entered the final day trailing 4-8, but swept three of the four triple-point matches on Championship Sunday to edge Andre Agassi's Team World 13-11 and return the trophy to European soil.

For Captain Yannick Noah, the triumph vindicated his aggressive Sunday selection. Placing four-time Grand Slam champion Carlos Alcaraz at the center of the action proved decisive, giving Europe the emotional spark needed to overcome a resilient World squad in front of 18,000 roaring spectators.

## What actually happened

Sky Sports and BBC Sport confirmed the dramatic sequence of events. The day opened with Alcaraz partnering Alexander Zverev to defeat Taylor Fritz and Alex de Minaur 6-3, 6-4 in doubles, immediately narrowing the deficit to 7-8. Team World responded when an inspired De Minaur outdueled Casper Ruud 7-6, 6-3, pushing Team World to the brink of victory at 11-7.

With zero room for error, Alexander Zverev took the court against Brandon Nakashima. The reigning US Open champion delivered 14 aces and saved three break points in a tense second-set tiebreak to win 6-4, 7-6, cutting the score to 10-11.

That set the stage for match 12: Carlos Alcaraz versus Taylor Fritz. Alcaraz unleashed his full repertoire of forehand winners and deft drop shots, breaking Fritz in the third game and taking the opener 6-2. Fritz rallied to lead 4-2 in the second, but Alcaraz raised his intensity, winning five of the final six games to seal a 6-2, 7-5 victory.

## The numbers that tell the story

- **Final score:** Team Europe 13, Team World 11.
- **Deciding match:** Alcaraz def. Fritz 6-2, 7-5 in one hour and 38 minutes.
- **Sunday surge:** Europe collected 9 of the 12 available points on Championship Sunday.
- **Decisive moments:** Alcaraz won 78% of his first-serve points and converted 4 of 6 break chances.
