The NFL stage shifts to Baltimore on Monday night, 16 November, as brothers Jim and John Harbaugh square off under the prime-time spotlight when the Los Angeles Chargers (6-3) take on the Baltimore Ravens (7-2) at M&T Bank Stadium. The matchup marks their first head-to-head meeting since Super Bowl XLVII, carrying immense postseason significance in an ultra-tight AFC conference race.

Jim Harbaugh's Chargers bring the NFL's third-ranked scoring defense (14.8 points allowed per game) into Baltimore, relying on Joey Bosa, Derwin James, and an ultra-disciplined ground game powered by J.K. Dobbins and quarterback Justin Herbert. Baltimore counters with two-time MVP Lamar Jackson and bruising tailback Derrick Henry, leading an offensive unit averaging 29.4 points per game. With division supremacy and head-to-head tiebreakers at stake, the sibling battle promises physical, four-quarter warfare.

The Monday night showcase follows an electric Sunday in Germany, where 70,225 fans at Munich's Allianz Arena watched Dan Campbell's Detroit Lions demolish the New England Patriots 35-10. Running back Jahmyr Gibbs put on an international clinic, accumulating 252 scrimmage yards (153 rushing, 99 receiving) and two touchdowns. Jared Goff completed 22 of 28 passes for 303 yards and three scores, including two to Amon-Ra St. Brown. The Patriots lost starting quarterback Drake Maye to an opening-quarter ankle injury on an Aidan Hutchinson sack, dropping New England to 5-5 while Detroit established its NFC heavyweight credentials at 7-2.
