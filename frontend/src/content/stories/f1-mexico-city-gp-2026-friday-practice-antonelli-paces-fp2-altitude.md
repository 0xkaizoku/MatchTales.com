## Italian prodigy masters thin air at Hermanos Rodriguez

Kimi Antonelli began his world championship coronation weekend in commanding style on Friday, 30 October, setting the fastest lap time during second practice for the 2026 Mexico City Grand Prix. Driving his Mercedes W17 through the thin mountain air at 2,285 meters above sea level, the 20-year-old sensation posted a lap of 1 minute 17.342 seconds on soft compound tires, edging Ferrari's Charles Leclerc by 0.112 seconds and Red Bull's Max Verstappen by 0.148 seconds.

The benchmark lap served as an emphatic statement following Antonelli's announcement on Thursday that he will retain his permanent car number 12 in 2027 rather than taking number 1. Antonelli carries a massive 123-point advantage over teammate George Russell into Sunday's 71-lap race, meaning a simple ninth-place finish (two points) will mathematically guarantee him the World Drivers' Championship, eclipsing Sebastian Vettel as the youngest champion in the 76-year history of Formula 1.

## High-altitude cooling and tire degradation dominate paddock

While Antonelli demonstrated supreme single-lap pace, teams up and down the pit lane wrestled with the severe aerodynamic and thermodynamic challenges posed by Mexico City's low atmospheric density. With cooling efficiency reduced by more than 20 percent, Mercedes and Red Bull fitted enlarged engine cover cooling gills, while brake duct temperatures soared during 15-lap race simulation runs in the afternoon sun.

Verstappen, a five-time victor in Mexico City, expressed optimism about Red Bull's long-run pace, noting that tire graining on the low-grip asphalt could create significant strategic variance between one-stop and two-stop approaches on Sunday. Meanwhile, Russell finished fourth in FP2, nearly three tenths adrift of Antonelli. As the legendary Foro Sol stadium section fills with passionate Mexican fans, all eyes remain firmly trained on the Italian phenom on the verge of sporting immortality.
