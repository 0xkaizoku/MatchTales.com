## Why this one matters

When the final whistles blew on Matchweek 7 on Sunday evening, the Premier League table told two radically contrasting stories at opposite ends of the spectrum.

At the summit, Manchester City enter the October international break standing completely unchallenged. Pep Guardiola's side have won all seven of their Premier League fixtures in 2026-27, accumulating 21 points with a commanding plus-10 goal difference. With Arsenal three points behind in second and Liverpool six points adrift in third, City's pursuit of yet another domestic crown is running with typical robotic precision.

At the bottom of the spectrum lies North London, where Ange Postecoglou and Tottenham Hotspur are engulfed in a full-blown existential crisis following an astonishing 3-2 collapse away at Brighton.

## Anatomy of an 18-minute meltdown

Tottenham's trip to the Amex Stadium on Sunday appeared destined for a routine away victory at halftime:

- **Total first-half control:** Brennan Johnson struck his sixth goal in as many games with a clinical 23rd-minute finish, and James Maddison doubled the lead eight minutes before the interval with a low strike through Bart Verbruggen's hands.
- **Hurzeler's halftime tactical coup:** Brighton's 31-year-old manager Fabian Hürzeler shifted his pressing triggers, pushing Kaoru Mitoma and Yankuba Minteh higher up the flanks to exploit Destiny Udogie and Pedro Porro.
- **The three-goal blitz:** Minteh pounced on Udogie's botched clearance in the 48th minute to pull one back. Ten minutes later, Georginio Rutter muscled through three Spurs defenders to slot home the equalizer. In the 66th minute, Danny Welbeck rose unmarked in the six-yard box to head home Rutter's cross, completing a stunning 18-minute, three-goal turnaround.
- **Spurs grim historical record:** The defeat means Tottenham have now dropped an alarming 10 points from winning positions in the league this season, leaving them rooted to 20th place with just two points from six matches.

## The international break reckoning

Postecoglou pulled no punches in his post-match assessment, calling the second half the worst performance of his tenure. With players now dispersing across the globe for UEFA Nations League fixtures and World Cup qualifiers, Tottenham will endure two agonizing weeks in the basement before facing West Ham on October 19.

For Brighton, the comeback vaults them into fourth place on 14 points, proving that Hürzeler's energetic, fearless brand of football can trouble anyone in England.
