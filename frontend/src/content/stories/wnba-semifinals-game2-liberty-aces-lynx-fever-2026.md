## Why this one matters

In the entire annals of WNBA postseason history, only one team has ever climbed out of an 0-2 deficit to win a best-of-five series. That 96 percent statistical wall looms over Tuesday night like an immovable fixture as the Las Vegas Aces and Indiana Fever take the court for Game 2 in New York and Minneapolis.

For the two-time defending champion Aces, Sunday's 87-77 loss at Barclays Center served as a harsh wake-up call. Breanna Stewart delivered a postseason masterpiece for the New York Liberty, tallying 34 points, eight rebounds, and five blocks while anchoring a defensive scheme that forced 18 Las Vegas turnovers. To avoid returning to Nevada in a critical hole, Becky Hammon's squad must reestablish offensive rhythm and provide MVP A'ja Wilson with cleaner entry angles against New York's frontcourt traps.

Two hours later at Target Center, the upstart Indiana Fever face an equally formidable task against the top-seeded Minnesota Lynx. While Minnesota took Game 1 88-82 behind Napheesa Collier's clinical 28-point performance, Caitlin Clark's 14-point fourth-quarter rally demonstrated that the Fever can penetrate Minnesota's half-court shell when their pace accelerates.

## Tactical keys to Game 2

Tuesday's doubleheader hinges on distinct tactical battlegrounds across both hardwood floors:

- **Controlling New York's transition attack:** In Game 1, the Liberty scored 22 fast-break points off Las Vegas turnovers. Becky Hammon stressed floor balance in Monday's shootaround, ordering guards Kelsey Plum and Jackie Young to retreat promptly rather than crashing the offensive glass.
- **Liberating A'ja Wilson:** When Wilson caught the ball in the low post on Sunday, Liberty defenders Jonquel Jones and Betnijah Laney-Hamilton collapsed immediately. Look for Las Vegas to utilize Wilson as a secondary playmaker at the foul line to trigger backdoor cuts.
- **Minnesota's perimeter containment:** Cheryl Reeve's Lynx stifled Indiana's shooters in the first half of Game 1, forcing Clark into five early turnovers. Christie Sides will counter with double-stagger screens beyond the arc to give Clark and Kelsey Mitchell daylight.
- **The Collier factor:** Napheesa Collier enters Tuesday averaging 31 points on 61 percent shooting in these playoffs. Indiana forward NaLyssa Smith will need defensive support from Aliyah Boston to prevent Collier from getting to her comfort zones in the mid-range.

## The championship stakes

Should New York and Minnesota protect home court on Tuesday, they will seize commanding 2-0 series advantages with three chances to punch tickets to the WNBA Finals.

For Las Vegas and Indiana, Tuesday is about pride, survival, and seizing home-court advantage before the series shift venues. The margin for error is officially zero.
