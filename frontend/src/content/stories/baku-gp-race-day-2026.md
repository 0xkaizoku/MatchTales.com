## Why this race matters

Formula 1 roars into race day on Saturday 26 September around the 6.003-kilometer Baku City Circuit. With Sunday designated for Azerbaijan's national Remembrance Day, the main event moves forward 24 hours, presenting drivers and strategists with an intense test on Baku's unforgiving asphalt.

## What actually happened

Formula1.com and Sky Sports confirm that rookie standout Kimi Antonelli captured pole position during Friday qualifying, posting a 1:40.445 lap time to lead an all-Mercedes front row alongside George Russell. Antonelli holds an 81-point championship lead with 292 points, but Baku has historically punished drivers who take safety for granted.

Ferrari's Charles Leclerc lines up third alongside McLaren's Lando Norris on row two, while Lewis Hamilton starts fifth. Baku's unique profile forces teams into extreme compromises: cars reach 350 km/h down the 2.2-kilometer Neftchilar Avenue straight before threading through the tight 7.6-meter Turn 8 pinch at the 12th-century Old City fortress walls.

Mercedes leads the constructors' standings on 503 points over Ferrari (358) and McLaren (306). However, high brake temperatures, sudden safety-car restarts, and unpredictable slipstreams regularly produce chaotic finishes along the Caspian shore.

## What to watch next

The 51-lap Grand Prix begins at 15:00 local time (12:00 BST). Can Antonelli convert his pole into another victory, or will Leclerc and Norris exploit Baku's slipstream straight to overturn the Mercedes advantage?
