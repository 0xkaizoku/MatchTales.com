Jim Harbaugh walked away with the ultimate family victory on Monday night, 16 November, as his Los Angeles Chargers produced a disciplined defensive stand to defeat John Harbaugh's Baltimore Ravens 23-20 at M&T Bank Stadium.

The highly anticipated Harbaugh Bowl, meeting for the first time since Super Bowl XLVII in 2013, lived up to its physical reputation from the opening snap. Justin Herbert delivered the contest's defining highlight early, escaping a collapsing pocket on third-and-goal and diving past two defenders for a 22-yard touchdown scramble. Los Angeles then leaned on rookie tailback Omarion Hampton, who churned out 105 rushing yards on 21 carries, including a punishing four-yard touchdown plunge that pushed the Chargers ahead 20-10 in the third quarter.

Baltimore answered with characteristic grit. Lamar Jackson engineered a fourth-quarter drive capped by a 14-yard touchdown strike to Mark Andrews, trimming the deficit to 23-20 with 4:18 remaining. With the Ravens driving toward field-goal range in the final two minutes, Chargers cornerback Tarheeb Still forced a fumble from receiver Zay Flowers, which Los Angeles recovered to extinguish the rally.

The victory improves the Chargers to 7-3 and hands Jim Harbaugh his first head-to-head victory over his older brother in the NFL. The Ravens drop to 7-3, suffering from a ground game that was shockingly neutralized: the Chargers outrushed Baltimore 160 yards to 16, holding superstar Derrick Henry to a season-low 12 yards on eight attempts.
