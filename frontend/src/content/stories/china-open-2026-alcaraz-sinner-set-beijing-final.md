## Why this one matters

The tournament organizers in Beijing could not have scripted a more perfect scenario. On Saturday afternoon at the National Tennis Center, Carlos Alcaraz and Jannik Sinner dismantled their respective semifinal opponents to establish the ultimate championship matchup in men's tennis.

Alcaraz got the fireworks started on Diamond Court with a tactical masterpiece against third seed Daniil Medvedev. The 21-year-old Spaniard mixed blazing baseline power with delicate drop volleys, frustrating Medvedev's deep returning position. Alcaraz won 82 percent of points on his first serve, broke Medvedev three times, and wrapped up a 7-5, 6-3 triumph in one hour and 28 minutes.

In the evening session, defending champion Jannik Sinner faced the emotional task of playing against China's breakthrough star Bu Yunchaokete in front of a capacity crowd. Sinner demonstrated the icy temperament that has propelled him to the top of the world rankings, firing 24 winners and closing out a 6-3, 7-6(3) victory.

## Chapter ten of a generational rivalry

Sunday's final marks the tenth meeting between Alcaraz and Sinner, with the Spaniard holding a narrow 5-4 head-to-head advantage. This match represents their first encounter since their epic five-set French Open semifinal in June.

- **Baseline warfare:** Both players hit with unmatched ball speed, but Alcaraz relies more on varied spin and court geometry, while Sinner generates relentless, flat pace from both wings.
- **Serving precision:** Sinner has dropped just one service game throughout the tournament, while Alcaraz has saved eight of nine break points faced this week.
- **Ranking implications:** With 500 ATP points on the line, Sinner can further cement his year-end World No. 1 status, while Alcaraz aims to close the gap before the Shanghai Masters.

On Diamond Court, tennis fans will witness the standard-bearers of modern tennis competing for high-stakes silverware.
