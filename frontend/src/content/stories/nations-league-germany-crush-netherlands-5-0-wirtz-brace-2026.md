Germany delivered a historic statement of attacking ruthlessness on Monday night, 16 November, crushing fierce rivals the Netherlands 5-0 at Red Bull Arena in Leipzig to secure top spot in UEFA Nations League Group A2.

The clash started at a blistering tempo, with Ronald Koeman's Oranje probing Germany's defensive shape in the early stages. But Julian Nagelsmann's men gradually took control of midfield through Florian Wirtz and Angelo Stiller. The breakthrough arrived on 41 minutes when 23-year-old winger Derry Scherhant gathered a diagonal ball, cut inside his marker, and drove a low angled strike past Bart Verbruggen.

The second half turned into a German procession. On the hour mark, Wirtz received possession 25 yards out, shifted the ball onto his right foot, and curled a sublime shot into the top corner. Five minutes later, Nico Schlotterbeck powered home a downward header from a corner to make it 3-0. Substitute Brajan Gruda added a fourth in the 72nd minute, before Wirtz capped his masterclass with a delicate chip over Verbruggen on 77 minutes.

The 5-0 defeat represents the Netherlands' heaviest margin of defeat in any international match since a 7-0 loss to West Germany in 1959. While both sides had already secured berths in the Nations League quarter-finals scheduled for March 2027, Germany advance as seeded group winners, leaving Koeman's squad facing fierce scrutiny over an alarming defensive collapse. Elsewhere in Nations League action on Monday, Greece edged Serbia 1-0 through Giorgos Masouras, while Switzerland dismantled Scotland 4-0 in Geneva.
